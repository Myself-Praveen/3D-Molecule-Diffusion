\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Drug discovery is a multi-billion dollar process that takes 10--15 years on average. Generative models that learn to produce novel, drug-like molecules from data can dramatically compress the early-stage search. The published work \textbf{GraphGANFed} (Manu et al., 2024) demonstrated that Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) can be trained in a \textit{Federated Learning} setting---enabling pharmaceutical companies to train collaboratively without sharing proprietary molecular data---but their model operates purely in 2D: it generates flat adjacency matrices and node-label vectors that describe molecular topology without spatial geometry.

This project extends GraphGANFed in a fundamental way: we \textbf{replace the 2D MLP-based GAN with a 3D E(3)-equivariant denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM)} that learns to generate true Cartesian coordinates and atom types from Gaussian noise. Our architecture uses an Equivariant Graph Neural Network (EGNN) as the denoiser, trained on the QM9 quantum-chemistry dataset of 133,000 small organic molecules. We replicate the federated protocol (IID and non-IID partitioning, FedAvg, FedProx) with this new generator, and further extend the system toward Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) targeted generation.

After stabilizing the baseline model and applying six validity-enhancing strategies (cosine noise schedules, model capacity escalation, equivariant attention gates, force-field relaxation, expanded $k$-NN neighborhoods, and quadratic DDIM stepping), our centralized baseline achieves \textbf{62.2\% validity} with $>$99\% uniqueness and novelty. We document every file, design decision, and experimental result in this report.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Motivation}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{The Drug Discovery Problem}

Traditional drug discovery follows a funnel: target identification $\to$ hit finding $\to$ lead optimization $\to$ preclinical studies $\to$ clinical trials. The hit-finding stage alone requires screening millions of candidate molecules, most of which fail. Generative models that can propose novel, synthesizable, drug-like molecules directly from learned chemical distributions offer a path to compress this stage from years to days.

\subsection{Why Federated Learning?}

Pharmaceutical companies hold proprietary molecular libraries that represent billions of dollars in R\&D investment. Traditional machine learning requires pooling all data in one location, which is commercially and legally impossible. \textbf{Federated Learning} (FL) solves this: each company trains a local model on its own data, and only model weight updates---not raw molecules---are shared with a central server. GraphGANFed \cite{manu2024graphganfed} was the first to apply FL to molecular generation, proving that this privacy-preserving paradigm works.

\subsection{Why Move from 2D to 3D?}

GraphGANFed represents molecules as \textit{2D adjacency matrices} $A \in \{0,1\}^{N \times N}$ and \textit{node-label vectors} $x \in \{C, N, O, \ldots\}^N$. This captures molecular \textit{topology} (which atoms are bonded) but not \textit{geometry} (where atoms sit in 3D space). However, molecular function---especially for drug targets like the Blood-Brain Barrier---depends critically on 3D shape:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Binding affinity} is governed by the lock-and-key fit of a molecule's 3D surface to a protein pocket.
    \item \textbf{BBB permeability} depends on molecular shape, polarity, and flexibility---properties that are defined in 3D.
    \item \textbf{Stereochemistry} (chirality, cis/trans isomers) is invisible to 2D representations but determines biological activity.
\end{itemize}

By replacing the 2D GAN with a \textbf{3D denoising diffusion model} that directly generates Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$ for every atom, our model can capture these geometric properties.

\subsection{Why Diffusion Instead of GAN?}

GANs suffer from well-known training instabilities: mode collapse (generator produces only a few molecule types), vanishing gradients (discriminator becomes too strong), and hyperparameter sensitivity. \textbf{Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models} (DDPMs) \cite{ho2020denoising} avoid these issues entirely:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{No adversarial dynamics:} the training objective is a simple mean-squared error between predicted and true noise.
    \item \textbf{Full distribution coverage:} diffusion models are trained to reverse a known Gaussian corruption process, avoiding mode collapse.
    \item \textbf{Stable training:} loss curves decrease monotonically; no generator-discriminator oscillation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Research Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{3D equivariant architecture:} First combination of EGNN-based 3D diffusion with the GraphGANFed federated protocol.
    \item \textbf{Joint coordinate and type diffusion:} Simultaneous Gaussian diffusion over coordinates with EDM-style categorical diffusion over atom types.
    \item \textbf{Federated 3D generation:} IID and non-IID partitioning of QM9 by molecular formula, trained with FedAvg and FedProx.
    \item \textbf{BBB-targeted generation:} Conditioned generation pipeline with a GCN-based BBB permeability oracle (val AUROC 0.92).
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive evaluation:} MOSES-style metrics plus connectivity analysis, force-field relaxation, and CNS MPO scoring.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Original Paper: GraphGANFed}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Paper Summary}

GraphGANFed \cite{manu2024graphganfed} (``A Federated Generative Framework for Graph-Structured Molecules Towards Efficient Drug Discovery,'' IEEE/ACM TCBB, 2024) proposes a federated GAN-based molecular generation framework. Key elements:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Molecular Representation:} Each molecule is a graph $G = (A, X)$ where $A$ is the adjacency matrix (binary) and $X$ is the node feature matrix (one-hot atom types).
    \item \textbf{Generator:} An MLP-based network within a Wasserstein GAN with Gradient Penalty (WGAN-GP) that takes random noise $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and outputs $(A, X)$.
    \item \textbf{Discriminator:} Classifies real vs.\ generated molecular graphs.
    \item \textbf{Federated Protocol:} $K$ clients each hold a private partition of molecules. In each round, the server broadcasts global weights; clients train locally for $E$ epochs; updated weights are aggregated via weighted FedAvg.
    \item \textbf{IID vs.\ Non-IID:} IID partitioning shuffles molecules randomly across clients. Non-IID partitioning groups molecules by molecular formula, simulating real-world pharmaceutical specialization.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What We Changed and Why}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Generator architecture:} MLP $\to$ E(3)-Equivariant Graph Neural Network (EGNN). MLPs are permutation-blind and cannot capture geometric relationships; EGNNs respect 3D rotational and translational symmetry.
    \item \textbf{Training paradigm:} GAN $\to$ DDPM. We avoid adversarial instability entirely; the diffusion loss is a simple MSE.
    \item \textbf{Output space:} 2D $(A, X)$ matrices $\to$ 3D coordinates $(x, y, z)^N$ and atom types $z^N$. This enables direct 3D molecular design.
    \item \textbf{Personalization:} Added FedProx ($\mu$-proximal term) and FedPer-style personal type/bond heads.
    \item \textbf{Target application:} Added BBB-targeted conditioned generation as a concrete drug-discovery use case.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why QM9? Dataset Rationale}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{What Is QM9?}

QM9 \cite{wu2018moleculenet} is a benchmark dataset of \textbf{133,885 small organic molecules} composed of up to 9 heavy atoms (C, N, O, F) plus hydrogen. For every molecule, QM9 provides:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{3D coordinates:} Quantum-mechanically optimized positions $(x_i, y_i, z_i)$ for every atom (DFT B3LYP/6-31G(2df,p) level of theory).
    \item \textbf{Atomic numbers:} Element identity $z_i \in \{1, 6, 7, 8, 9\}$.
    \item \textbf{Molecular properties:} Dipole moment, HOMO/LUMO energies, heat capacity, etc.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why QM9 Is the Right Choice}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Consistency with GraphGANFed:} The original paper used QM9, so using the same dataset enables direct comparison.
    \item \textbf{3D ground truth:} QM9 is one of the few molecular datasets with \textit{quantum-mechanically computed} 3D coordinates, not heuristic conformer generators. This is essential for training a 3D diffusion model.
    \item \textbf{Community benchmark:} QM9 is the standard evaluation dataset for all major 3D molecular generative models (EDM, E-NFs, GeoLDM, GeoDiff), enabling comparison with state-of-the-art.
    \item \textbf{Chemical diversity:} Despite the $\leq$9 heavy-atom constraint, QM9 contains 133K distinct molecules spanning alcohols, amines, ethers, ketones, heterocycles, and fluorinated species.
    \item \textbf{Tractable size:} At 133K molecules, QM9 is large enough for statistical learning but small enough for CPU-only training within academic compute budgets.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Foundations}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM)}

A DDPM \cite{ho2020denoising} defines a \textbf{forward process} that gradually corrupts data $\mathbf{x}_0$ by adding Gaussian noise over $T$ timesteps:
\begin{equation}
    q(\mathbf{x}_t | \mathbf{x}_0) = \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{x}_t; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\mathbf{x}_0,\; (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t)\,\mathbf{I}\right)
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t} (1 - \beta_s)$ and $\{\beta_t\}_{t=1}^T$ is a variance schedule.

The \textbf{reverse process} is a neural network $\epsilon_\theta$ trained to predict the noise $\epsilon$ that was added:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_\text{simple} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{x}_0, \epsilon}\left[ \|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)\|^2 \right]
\end{equation}

\subsection{Centered Coordinate Diffusion}

For molecular coordinates, we enforce \textbf{translation invariance} by centering the noise per molecule:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\epsilon}_i = \epsilon_i - \frac{1}{N_m} \sum_{j \in \text{mol}(i)} \epsilon_j
\end{equation}
This ensures $\sum_{j \in \text{mol}} \tilde{\epsilon}_j = \mathbf{0}$, so the center of mass stays at the origin throughout the diffusion process. Implemented in \texttt{CenteredDDPM.add\_noise()}.

\subsection{Categorical Type Diffusion}

For discrete atom types $z \in \{1, 6, 7, 8, 9\}$, we use the EDM-style categorical process \cite{hoogeboom2022equivariant}:
\begin{equation}
    q(z_t | z_0) = \text{Cat}\left(\bar{\alpha}_t \cdot \text{onehot}(z_0) + \frac{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}{K}\right)
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the number of atom classes. At $t=0$ the distribution is concentrated on the true type; at $t=T$ it approaches uniform. The reverse posterior $q(z_{t-1} | z_t, \hat{z}_0)$ is computed analytically in \texttt{TypeDDPM.posterior\_probs()}.

\subsection{Noise Schedules}

\subsubsection{Linear Schedule}
The default schedule linearly interpolates $\beta_t$ from $\beta_\text{start}=10^{-4}$ to $\beta_\text{end}=0.02$:
\begin{equation}
    \beta_t = \beta_\text{start} + \frac{t-1}{T-1}(\beta_\text{end} - \beta_\text{start})
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Cosine Schedule (Strategy 2)}
The improved DDPM cosine schedule \cite{nichol2021improved} defines $\bar{\alpha}_t$ directly:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\alpha}_t = \frac{f(t)}{f(0)}, \quad f(t) = \cos^2\!\left(\frac{t/T + s}{1 + s} \cdot \frac{\pi}{2}\right)
\end{equation}
with $s = 0.008$. This preserves more signal at intermediate timesteps, giving the denoiser more ``time budget'' for fine bond-length precision.

\subsection{DDIM Sampling}

Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) \cite{song2021denoising} enable sampling with fewer than $T$ steps by defining a deterministic mapping:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_{t-1} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{x}}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t-1} - \sigma_t^2} \cdot \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t) + \sigma_t \cdot \epsilon
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_t = \eta \cdot \sqrt{(1-\bar\alpha_{t-1})/(1-\bar\alpha_t)} \cdot \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t/\bar\alpha_{t-1}}$ and $\eta \in [0, 1]$ controls stochasticity.

\subsection{E(3)-Equivariant Graph Neural Networks}

An EGNN \cite{satorras2021en} updates both node features $\mathbf{h}_i$ and coordinates $\mathbf{x}_i$ while preserving equivariance under the Euclidean group $E(3)$ (rotations, reflections, translations):
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{m}_{ij} &= \phi_e\!\left(\mathbf{h}_i, \mathbf{h}_j, \|\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j\|^2, \mathbf{t}\right) \\
    \Delta\mathbf{x}_i &= \sum_{j \neq i} (\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j) \cdot \phi_x(\mathbf{m}_{ij}) \\
    \mathbf{h}_i' &= \mathbf{h}_i + \phi_h\!\left(\mathbf{h}_i, \sum_j \mathbf{m}_{ij}, \mathbf{t}\right)
\end{align}
The coordinate update $\Delta\mathbf{x}_i$ is along the direction $\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j$, scaled by a learned function of the message---this is what makes it equivariant: rotating the input rotates the output identically.

\subsection{Detailed File Descriptions}

\subsubsection{\texttt{src/models/egnn.py} --- The Denoiser Architecture}

This is the \textbf{core neural network} of the entire project. It implements:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{timestep\_embedding()}: Sinusoidal positional encoding for diffusion timesteps, mapping integer $t$ to a continuous vector of dimension \texttt{time\_dim}. Uses the same frequency formula as the Transformer \cite{vaswani2017attention}.
    
    \item \texttt{EGNNLayer} (lines 34--104): A single message-passing layer that:
    \begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
        \item Computes edge messages from concatenated node features, squared distances, and timestep embeddings.
        \item Optionally applies a \textbf{sigmoid attention gate} (Strategy 4) to distinguish bonded from merely-close neighbors.
        \item Updates coordinates equivariantly along inter-atomic direction vectors.
        \item Updates node features via an MLP over aggregated messages.
        \item Uses \textbf{zero-initialization} on the coordinate update head (so the untrained network is the identity map).
    \end{enumerate}
    
    \item \texttt{EquivariantGenerator} (lines 107--236): The full denoiser model:
    \begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
        \item Embeds atom types via \texttt{nn.Embedding}.
        \item Projects timestep embeddings through a 2-layer MLP.
        \item (Optional) Adds property conditioning for BBB-targeted generation.
        \item Stacks $L$ \texttt{EGNNLayer}s for message passing.
        \item Outputs predicted noise $\hat{\epsilon}$ (coordinate head), type logits (type head), and bond logits (bond head).
    \end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Design rationale:} The noise prediction is computed as $\hat{\epsilon} = \mathbf{x}_\text{updated} - \mathbf{x}_\text{input}$, requiring the critical \texttt{clone()} call on line 220. This bug (aliased tensors producing zero gradients) was the single most impactful discovery of the project (see Section~\ref{sec:discoveries}).

\subsubsection{\texttt{src/models/diffusion.py} --- Noise Schedules}

Implements the forward noising processes:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{\_cosine\_betas()}: Computes the cosine schedule in float64 precision to avoid numerical cancellation near $t=0$.
    \item \texttt{CenteredDDPM}: Gaussian diffusion over 3D coordinates with mandatory per-molecule center-of-mass subtraction. The \texttt{add\_noise()} method broadcasts per-molecule timesteps to per-atom noise levels.
    \item \texttt{TypeDDPM}: Categorical diffusion with \texttt{forward\_probs()}, \texttt{sample\_noisy\_types()}, and \texttt{posterior\_probs()} implementing the EDM categorical process.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{\texttt{src/sampling.py} --- Reverse Samplers}

The \texttt{sample\_molecules()} function (lines 49--189) generates molecules from pure noise:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Initialize coordinates $\mathbf{x}_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ (re-centered) and types $z_T \sim \text{Uniform}(K)$.
    \item For each reverse step: build $k$-NN graph, predict noise, update coordinates via DDPM or DDIM, update types via categorical posterior.
    \item Apply numerical guards: logit clamping at $\pm 15$, bulletproof multinomial fallback for NaN/Inf rows.
    \item Return centered coordinates and type indices.
\end{enumerate}

Supports \textbf{classifier-free guidance} (lines 121--127) and \textbf{quadratic DDIM stepping} (Strategy 6) via \texttt{ddim\_timestep\_grid()}.

\subsubsection{\texttt{src/fed/partition.py} --- Data Partitioning}

Replicates the GraphGANFed data distribution protocol:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{molecular\_formula()}: Converts atomic number vectors to Hill-notation formulas (e.g., $[6,6,8,1,1,1] \to$ ``C2H3O'').
    \item \texttt{partition\_iid()}: Class-stratified equal split---every client sees the same proportion of each formula.
    \item \texttt{partition\_niid()}: Dirichlet($\alpha$)-based unbalanced allocation---some clients get mostly nitrogen-containing molecules, others mostly carbon-only, simulating real pharmaceutical specialization.
    \item Partitions are cached to \texttt{data/partitions/K\{K\}\_\{mode\}.json} for reproducibility.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{\texttt{src/fed/server.py} --- Federated Server}

Implements the round-based federated aggregation loop:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Broadcast global model weights to all clients.
    \item Each client runs $E$ local training epochs.
    \item Aggregate updated weights via weighted FedAvg: $w_\text{global} = \sum_k \frac{n_k}{n} w_k$.
    \item Log per-round metrics to \texttt{history.json}.
    \item Periodic DDIM-based validity probes for geometry health monitoring.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{\texttt{src/fed/trainer.py} --- Local Client Training}

Each client runs:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Standard diffusion training: sample $t$, add noise, predict $\hat{\epsilon}$, backprop MSE.
    \item \textbf{FedProx proximal term}: $\frac{\mu}{2}\|w - w_\text{global}\|^2$ added to the loss (configurable $\mu$).
    \item \textbf{Personal heads}: Type and bond classification heads stay local (not aggregated), allowing clients to specialize.
    \item Gradient clipping + NaN skip guards for numerical stability.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{\texttt{src/objectives.py} --- Multi-Objective Losses}

Implements differentiable chemistry-aware losses beyond the standard noise MSE:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $\lambda_2$ \textbf{valence penalty}: Penalizes atoms whose expected bond count exceeds their chemical valence. Evaluated on the model's predicted clean coordinates $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0$ (not noisy $\mathbf{x}_t$) with a timestep gate ($t < \tau$) to avoid penalizing meaningless high-noise predictions.
    \item $\lambda_3$ \textbf{diversity regularizer}: Penalizes pairwise cosine similarity of mean-pooled molecular embeddings within a batch, encouraging the generator to produce varied molecules.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{\texttt{src/utils/evaluation.py} --- Metrics Suite}

Implements the complete MOSES evaluation protocol plus extensions:

Bond reconstruction uses \textbf{element-aware covalent radii} (Cordero et al., 2008): a pair is bonded iff $d_{ij} \leq r_i + r_j + 0.45$~\AA. This replaced a naive distance cutoff that failed even on ground-truth QM9 (0.7\% valid at 2.5~\AA).

\subsubsection{\texttt{src/utils/graph.py} --- k-NN Graph Construction}

A single vectorized \texttt{torch.cdist} call over all atoms, with cross-molecule and self-loop masking to infinity before top-$k$ selection. Purely PyTorch---no optional \texttt{pyg-lib} binary backend required.

\subsubsection{\texttt{train.py} --- Centralized Training Loop}

The main training script orchestrates:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item YAML config loading, seed setting, device selection.
    \item QM9 loading with 80/10/10 train/val/test split.
    \item Model, optimizer (Adam), scheduler (CosineAnnealing) initialization.
    \item Per-batch: sample $t$, add centered Gaussian noise, build $k$-NN graph, predict noise, compute $L = L_\text{pos} + \lambda_\text{type} L_\text{type} + \lambda_2 L_\text{valence} + \lambda_3 L_\text{diversity}$.
    \item Early stopping on validation loss with patience.
    \item Periodic \texttt{quick\_valid} probes: generate 16 molecules via DDIM-20 and report validity.
    \item \textbf{Resumable:} \texttt{--resume --run\_epochs N} loads \texttt{last.pt} (atomic writes prevent corruption).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{\texttt{fed\_train.py} --- Federated Training Entry Point}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash, caption={Federated training commands}]
python fed_train.py --config configs/fed_iid.yaml    # IID, FedAvg
python fed_train.py --config configs/fed_niid.yaml   # non-IID, FedProx
\end{lstlisting}

Partitions QM9 by molecular formula, creates per-client data loaders, initializes \texttt{LocalTrainer}s, and delegates to \texttt{FederatedServer} for the round-based aggregation loop.

\subsubsection{\texttt{generate\_and\_eval.py} --- Evaluation Pipeline}

End-to-end generation and evaluation:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Load trained checkpoint (supports both central and federated formats).
    \item Sample atom counts from the training histogram.
    \item Generate molecules in batches via DDIM.
    \item (Optional) Apply force-field relaxation (\texttt{--relax}).
    \item Reconstruct RDKit molecules from 3D coordinates.
    \item Compute all MOSES + extended metrics.
    \item Save \texttt{metrics.json}, \texttt{smiles.txt}, \texttt{molecules.sdf}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Key Discoveries During Development}
\label{sec:discoveries}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Discovery A: The Dead Coordinate Head}

The most critical bug in the original codebase: \texttt{forward()} computed \texttt{initial\_pos = pos} without \texttt{.clone()}, while \texttt{encode()} rebinds \texttt{pos} locally. This made \texttt{noise\_pred = updated\_pos - initial\_pos} collapse to exactly $\mathbf{0}$ with no gradient path. \textbf{Every model trained before this fix learned only atom types---coordinates were random.}

\textbf{Fix:} \texttt{initial\_pos = pos.clone()} (one-line fix, line 220 of \texttt{egnn.py}).

\subsection{Discovery B: Atom Index Convention}

PyG's QM9 loader stores raw atomic numbers in \texttt{data.z}: $\{1, 6, 7, 8, 9\}$ for $\{$H, C, N, O, F$\}$. The original evaluation code applied a secondary lookup table that shifted every element (H$\to$C, C$\to$S, etc.), fabricating validity numbers. \textbf{Fix:} The model's type index IS the atomic number---identity mapping.

\subsection{Discovery C: The Evaluation Lens Was Broken}

The original \texttt{coords\_and\_types\_to\_mol()} used a flat 2.5~\AA{} distance cutoff for bond assignment, which failed even on ground-truth QM9 (0.7\% valid). Methyl H$\cdots$H contacts ($\sim$1.78~\AA) and 1-3 ring C$\cdots$C contacts ($\sim$2.42~\AA) were spuriously bonded, violating valence rules. \textbf{Fix:} Element-aware covalent radii (bond iff $d \leq r_i + r_j + 0.45$~\AA). Ground truth validity: $99.0\%$.

\section{Six Strategies for 80\% Validity}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Our baseline (128-dim, 6-layer, linear schedule, DDIM-200/eta0.5) achieves 47.1\% raw validity. We identified six strategies from the 3D generation literature to push toward 80\%:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{clcl}
\toprule
\textbf{S\#} & \textbf{Strategy} & \textbf{Retrain?} & \textbf{Implementation} \\
\midrule
1 & Force-field relaxation (MMFF94/UFF) & No & \texttt{--relax} flag \\
2 & Cosine noise schedule & Yes & \texttt{schedule: cosine} \\
3 & 256-dim / 8-layer capacity & Yes & \texttt{central\_v3\_full.yaml} \\
4 & Equivariant attention gates & Yes & \texttt{use\_attention: true} \\
5 & $k$-NN = 8 neighborhood & Yes & \texttt{kNN: 8} \\
6 & Quadratic DDIM stepping & No & \texttt{--step\_schedule quadratic} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{S1: Force-Field Relaxation}
Post-hoc energy minimization snaps near-miss bond lengths into chemically valid distances. Uses RDKit's MMFF94 force field with UFF fallback. No retraining needed---applied at evaluation time.

\subsection{S2: Cosine Noise Schedule}
The cosine schedule (Nichol \& Dhariwal, 2021) preserves more signal at intermediate timesteps compared to the linear schedule, giving the denoiser more ``budget'' for fine-grained bond-length precision.

\subsection{S3: Model Capacity Escalation}
Doubling \texttt{node\_dim} from 128 to 256 and increasing layers from 6 to 8 raises the parameter count from 0.78M to 4.09M, providing the representational power needed for precise 3D coordinate prediction.

\subsection{S4: Equivariant Attention Gates}
A per-edge sigmoid gate $a_{ij} = \sigma(\text{MLP}(\mathbf{m}_{ij}))$ re-weights edge messages before aggregation, allowing the model to distinguish bonded neighbors from merely-close steric contacts.

\subsection{S5: $k$-NN = 8 Expansion}
Doubling the neighborhood from 4 to 8 gives each atom access to more geometric context per message-passing step.

\subsection{S6: Quadratic DDIM Stepping}
A quadratic timestep grid $t_i = (i/S)^2 \cdot (T-1)$ concentrates steps at low noise levels where bond-length precision is decided. No retraining needed.

\subsection{BBB Oracle}

\subsection{Conditioned Architecture}

The EGNN accepts an optional conditioning vector:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{t}_\text{cond} = \mathbf{t}_\text{emb} + \text{MLP}(\text{Embed}(\text{label}) \mathbin\| [\text{QED}, \text{LogP}, \text{tPSA}, \text{MW}])

With classifier-free guidance at inference ($w > 0$):
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\epsilon}_\text{guided} = \hat{\epsilon}_\text{uncond} + w \cdot (\hat{\epsilon}_\text{cond} - \hat{\epsilon}_\text{uncond})
\end{equation}

\subsection{Baseline BBB Rate}

Unconditioned generation achieves \textbf{24.8\%} BBB permeability on 471 valid molecules---the target for conditioned generation to exceed.

\section{Lab-Readiness Assessment: Six Stages and a Gate}
\label{sec:labready}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The honest-ruler metrics answer ``is this a connected, sanitizable
molecule?''. A synthesis lab asks a stricter question: \emph{can a chemist
order this and make it?} The lab-readiness stack answers that in six
independently usable stages (\texttt{src/utils/chem\_qa.py},
\texttt{src/utils/retrosynthesis.py}), each labelled for what it is rather
than for what a literature name would imply:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{2.6cm}p{5.4cm}p{6.2cm}}
\toprule
Stage & Measures & What it is (and is not) \\
\midrule
1 Perception & bond orders and stereochemistry read back from the 3D
coordinates & perception, not repair: it never adds or removes bonds, so
ConnectedValidity is untouched \\
2 Geometry & bond-length strain against covalent radii (Cordero 2008) and
MMFF94/UFF strain energy & deviation from covalent norms, not a free
energy \\
3 Synthesis & 1--10 complexity proxy + PAINS catalogue matches & the
complexity score is explicitly \emph{not} the published Ertl SA score (no
fitted fragment table is shipped); PAINS is RDKit's real Baell--Holloway
catalogue \\
4 Domain & nearest-training-neighbour Tanimoto (Morgan r2/2048) & the same
fingerprint family as SNN, so similarities are comparable across metrics \\
5 ADMET & ESOL solubility + hERG/CYP/P-gp structural alerts & exact
published Delaney coefficients; the alerts are auditable SMARTS patterns,
not trained predictors --- no IC$_{50}$, no probability \\
6 Routes & bounded template retrosynthesis from common building blocks &
net transformations within a 2-step horizon, not a production
retrosynthesis engine \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Stage~7 (\texttt{src/utils/lab\_gate.py}) runs all six over one cohort and
renders a single PASS/FAIL/INCOMPLETE verdict from data-driven criteria
(metric, operator, threshold and a one-line rationale for each, all
replaceable by the caller):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llrl}
\toprule
Criterion & Metric & Threshold & Needs \\
\midrule

\end{tabular}

2-step template horizon routes only \textbf{0.74\%} of the 1628 real BBBP
training compounds (purchasable drugs are not 2-step builds), while
QM9-size molecules route near-100\% --- no absolute threshold serves both
datasets, and a threshold real chemistry itself fails would be meaningless.

\section{Corrected Geometry Plan: Interventions and Diagnostics}
\label{sec:geometry_plan}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Sections above establish the BBB geometry failure empirically. This section
records the corrected intervention suite built against it, the two errors in the
working diagnosis that the intervention work exposed, and the measurement
protocol used to judge each arm. All code described here ships default-off: no
existing recipe, checkpoint or reported number changes unless a flag is passed.

\subsection{Two Errors in the Working Diagnosis}

\textbf{1. A bond-count unit mismatch.} Throughout the earlier analysis, the
BBB arm was described as producing "32 bonds per molecule against ${\sim}19$
for real BBBP". Those two numbers were measured in different units: the
evaluation ruler counts \emph{every} bond, including C--H, while 19 is a
heavy-atom figure. Measured with the identical ruler on both sides
(\texttt{scripts/diagnose\_bbb\_clump.py}, 200 real training molecules as the
reference): real BBBP sits at \textbf{26.88 bonds per molecule} and
\textbf{0.999 bonds per atom}, where the generated arms produce 0.81
(bbb\_full) and 0.93 (V5) bonds per atom. The generated molecules are
therefore not twice over-bonded --- their local bonding is close to the
reference and slightly \emph{below} it. This matters directly for RL: a reward
that penalises $|\text{bonds} - 19|$ on H-inclusive molecules would push the
model away from correct geometry, so \texttt{REFERENCE\_BONDS\_PER\_MOL}
(26.88) is the number exposed in \texttt{src/rl/rewards.py}.

\textbf{2. The failure is fragmentation, not fusion.} The same diagnostic
separates the two physically distinct ways a molecule can be "soup":

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
 & bonds/atom & fragments/mol & largest-frag. frac & $R_{gy}$ (\AA{}) & single-frag \% \\
\midrule
real BBBP (ref.)   & 0.999 & 1.04 & 0.997 & 3.09--3.43 & 97--98 \\
bbb\_full best     & 0.813 & 8.98 & 0.296 & 7.98       & 2.5  \\
V5 baseline        & 0.928 & 5.91 & 0.740 & 3.75       & 20.1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A fused clump would show \emph{few} fragments, a small radius of gyration and
an elevated bond density. None of that holds: the generated molecules are
dense local clusters scattered through space --- eight or nine of them per
molecule for the full-budget arm --- at a radius of gyration more than twice
the reference. The model reproduces bond-length-scale geometry locally and
fails to assemble one connected object. This reframes the target of the
interventions: what has to change is how fragments are stitched and how far
apart they are placed, not how hard atoms are pushed apart.

\subsection{Test-Time Steric Guidance (Intervention 1)}

Nothing in the objective or the sampler constrains non-bonded proximity, so the
plan's first intervention adds one at test time with no retraining. The energy
is a per-element-pair hard sphere evaluated on the \emph{clean} estimate
$\hat{x}_0$:

\begin{equation}
    E(\hat{x}_0) = \tfrac{1}{2} k \sum_{i \ne j}
        \big[\max\big(0,\ \sigma_{ij} - d_{ij}\big)\big]^2,
    \qquad \sigma_{ij} = s\,(r_i^{\mathrm{cov}} + r_j^{\mathrm{cov}}),
\end{equation}

with $s = 0.75$ by default, and the guided step
$\hat{x}_0 \leftarrow \hat{x}_0 - w\,\nabla_{\hat{x}_0} E$ followed by the
normal reverse update. Three corrections over the plan as first written:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Real bonds must feel zero force.} The proposed clash window
          $1.2 < d < 1.8$~\AA{} contains every real covalent bond (C--C 1.54,
          C--N 1.47, C--O 1.43, C=C 1.34, C=O 1.22~\AA{}), so it repels
          correct chemistry while applying no force to a true overlap at
          0.5--0.9~\AA{}. The element-pair form gives $\sigma_{\mathrm{CC}} =
          1.14$~\AA{} $<$ every real C--C bond and $\sigma_{\mathrm{CH}} =
          0.81$~\AA{} $<$ every real C--H bond. The plan's flat
          $r_{clash} = 1.1$~\AA{} threshold is available as an \emph{additive}
          term (\texttt{--steric\_r\_clash}) but cannot be the only term: it
          lands inside real C--H bonds at 1.06--1.10~\AA{}.
    \item \textbf{Guidance acts on $\hat{x}_0$, not $x_t$.} At $t > 0$ the
          coordinates still carry diffusion noise, so penalising clashes there
          punishes the noise draw itself (DPS/Tweedie guidance). No extra model
          forward pass is needed --- the energy is a pure function of the
          coordinates --- so the plan's redundant \texttt{model(...)} call
          inside the autograd block is dropped.
    \item \textbf{The schedule must index the sampling progress.} Reverse
          diffusion counts $t$ down while the loop counter counts up; gating on
          both a small $t$ and a late progress fraction is unsatisfiable. The
          weight is a linear ramp \texttt{steric\_guidance\_weight(step\_idx,
          N, ramp\_start, max\_weight)} that starts at 70\% of sampling and
          peaks at the final step --- a ramp also avoids injecting a step
          discontinuity exactly where bond lengths are settled.
\end{itemize}

Everything is off by default: \texttt{--steric\_weight 0} is a bit-exact
reproduction of the legacy sampler (regression-tested), with
\texttt{--steric\_enabled}, \texttt{--steric\_max\_weight},
\texttt{--steric\_k\_rep}, \texttt{--steric\_ramp\_start} and
\texttt{--steric\_sigma\_scale} as the plan's test-matrix knobs.

\subsection{Coordinate Temperature and the Fragment Fallback (Intervention 2)}

\texttt{--coord\_temperature} adds a per-step Gaussian perturbation scaled by
the noise level the trajectory just landed on (the coordinate analogue of the
existing type temperature), and \texttt{--keep\_largest\_fragment} implements
the plan's post-hoc spatial-clustering fallback by keeping only the largest
distance-graph component. Both are default-off and both are labelled honestly
in the tooling: the fragment filter relabels a molecule's extent rather than
fixing generation, so it is a diagnostic knob, never a headline setting.

\subsection{Radius-Aware Coordinate Normalization (Intervention 3)}

The same absolute noise scale was applied to a 9-atom QM9 molecule and a
40-atom BBBP one. \texttt{CenteredDDPM} now optionally runs the entire
forward and reverse process in size-normalized coordinates,

\begin{equation}
    x_0 \mapsto x_0 / R(N), \qquad R(N) = c\, N^{1/3},
\end{equation}

with $c = 1.06$~\AA{} --- the measured median of
$R_{rms} / N^{1/3}$ over the BBBP training set (mean $R_{rms}$ 3.59~\AA{} at
mean $N = 40.9$, $n = 600$), not a tuned constant. Depending only on the atom
count is what makes the transform runnable at generation time; the plan's
version scaled noise by the \emph{ground-truth conformer radius}, a quantity
that does not exist for a molecule being generated. The arm ships as
\texttt{configs/central\_v6\_bbb\_radius.yaml} (V5 verbatim plus the two
diffusion keys), carries a resume guard because the coordinate scale is baked
into the weights, and denormalizes $\hat{x}_0$ before the \AA{}-thresholded
valence and geometry penalties so their cutoffs stay in the right units. A
1-epoch smoke run into a temporary checkpoint directory verified the full
path: trains, samples, records the setting, and the generator announces the
normalization it sampled under.

\subsection{RAFT Fallback (Step 1.5)}

If test-time guidance lands only in the 4--6\% band, the plan directs a
rejection-sampling pass \emph{before} any preference optimization --- learning
from atom soup teaches the model to reward atom soup.
\texttt{scripts/raft\_finetune.py} filters candidates on validity,
connectivity and a bond window around the measured reference (the plan's
15--25 around 19 rescaled to this ruler), reports how many candidates failed
each criterion, refuses to run below \texttt{--min\_elites}, and then performs
a plain supervised MSE pass on the survivors, writing a checkpoint in
\texttt{train.py}'s format. Measured on the V5 baseline SDF: 174 candidates
$\rightarrow$ 139 fragmented, 14 outside the bond window, \textbf{21 elites},
with a two-epoch smoke fine-tune taking the elite loss from 0.650 to 0.532.

\subsection{Measurement Protocol}

Every arm in this section is measured under one protocol so the rows are
comparable: 1000 (or 200--400 for sweeps) samples, DDIM-200, $\eta = 1.0$,
quadratic step schedule, seed 42, \texttt{--relax}, the covalent-radii bond
ruler, and the clump diagnostic run alongside each output. The chains that
produce them are committed (\texttt{scripts/chain\_bbb\_geometry\_arms.sh},
\texttt{scripts/chain\_bbb\_final\_eval.sh}) and are resumable per arm.

the RAFT path stays available but unused.

\subsection{A Silent BBB\% Zeroing Bug}

The oracle's batched scorer inferred each graph's node count from
\texttt{edge\_index}, for which PyG takes \texttt{max() + 1}. A molecule whose
highest-index atom has no neighbour inside the 2.5~\AA{} distance gate
therefore collated one atom short, the collated atom-type and batch vectors
disagreed, and the pooling layer raised. A blanket \texttt{except} returned
zeros for the entire call, so one such molecule anywhere in a cohort reported
\textbf{BBB\% $=$ 0.0} for all of it, with no log line. It was caught by
comparing a chain summary against per-arm \texttt{metrics.json}: a 398-valid
cohort cannot have zero BBB-positives at a 33\% base rate.

The fix sets \texttt{num\_nodes} explicitly and, if a batched forward still
fails, falls back to per-molecule scoring with a warning instead of returning
zeros; three regression tests pin the properties. Only the pre-RL row was
re-scored from its saved SDF (0.0 $\rightarrow$ 34.2\%, provenance recorded in
the directory's \texttt{metrics.json}), because re-scoring every arm moves
correct rows by $-3.6$ to $+1.9$ points: the evaluator re-embeds each molecule
with ETKDG instead of scoring the conformer the model produced, so BBB\%
carries that embedding's noise. \textbf{BBB\% differences below ${\sim}4$
points at $n \approx 100$ are not signal}, and switching the evaluator to the
zero-shift generated-conformer path --- which the reward module's documentation
already assumes --- is a re-measurement task, not a patch.

\section{Preference Fine-Tuning (Phase 2): Design and Guard}
\label{sec:rl}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Phase 2 refines BBB steering on top of a geometry that has already been fixed;
RL does not fix geometry, and the project's standing rule is that no preference
optimization runs before the Phase 1 gate passes. The stack ships therefore
with \texttt{rl.enabled: false} in every config.

\textbf{Reward (}\texttt{src/rl/rewards.py}\textbf{).} A composite of BBB
probability, validity, QED, connectivity, bond-count distance, severe clashes
and fragment count using the plan's weights. Invalid molecules return exactly
$-1.0$ rather than being dropped: they are the \emph{rejected} side of a
preference pair, and the failure mode the model must stop producing is exactly
an invalid clump. The oracle is loaded as a plain \texttt{nn.Module}
(manual \texttt{torch.load} + \texttt{load\_state\_dict}; no
Lightning-style \texttt{load\_from\_checkpoint} or \texttt{predict\_proba})
and scored through the evaluation's own zero-shift geometry path.

\textbf{Pairing (}\texttt{src/rl/preference\_pairs.py}\textbf{).} Two
constructors: \emph{seed-matched} pairs rollouts drawn from the same initial
noise under two guidance scales ($w = 4.0$ vs $w = 0.0$), so the only
difference inside a pair is what guidance did; \emph{valid-vs-invalid} zips the
best valid molecules against invalid clumps. Samples keep their generated
$(\mathbf{r}, z)$ even when molecule construction failed, so rejected examples
remain encodable for the diffusion loss.

\textbf{DPO (}\texttt{src/rl/dpo.py}\textbf{).} Three corrections are pinned
by tests: the implicit reward is
$\mathrm{MSE}_{ref} - \mathrm{MSE}_{model}$ (higher when the fine-tuned model
denoises better) and the loss is $-\log\sigma\big(\beta(r_{chosen} -
r_{rejected})\big)$, i.e. the model maximises its advantage over a frozen
reference --- the opposite of the sign that would train it on the losers; each
side is noised with \texttt{add\_noise} at its own random timestep before the
forward pass, because the denoiser only ever sees $x_t$; and the reference runs
under \texttt{no\_grad} with parameters frozen. The flow objective is refused
explicitly rather than silently mis-scored.

\textbf{Wiring (}\texttt{train.py}\textbf{)} adds an opt-in \texttt{rl}
block: \texttt{init\_checkpoint} (or \texttt{--resume}) sets the fine-tuning
start, \texttt{pair\_file} feeds pairs from \texttt{scripts/build\_rl\_pairs.py},
\texttt{dpo\_ref\_checkpoint} anchors the reference, and \texttt{dpo\_lr} /
\texttt{dpo\_epochs} take over the stage's optimizer and budget. The stage
joined the resume guard, since it changes what the weights are optimised
against. End-to-end smoke on the V5 checkpoint: a pair file generated from six
seed-matched rollouts (three pairs accepted, rejected side 100\% invalid
clumps), a one-epoch stage starting from the pre-RL weights with a frozen
reference, logging \texttt{dpo=0.6870} and exiting cleanly.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Each limitation below was established by direct measurement rather than
conjecture, and together they bound what the reported results support.

\subsection{L1: BBB-Targeted Generation Does Not Yet Produce Molecules}

\textbf{The headline BBB claim is not met.} Every centralized, federated,
ablated and guidance-swept arm run against the full-budget
(\texttt{bbb\_full}, 200k-step) checkpoint produces unconnected atom soup, with
\texttt{ConnectedValidity} (valid \emph{and} a single connected fragment)
equal to \textbf{0.0} in every one of those configurations. The later V5
retrain does not reproduce that exact zero --- it reaches 8.75--14.0\%
(\S\ref{sec:geometry_plan}) --- but the same failure survives it, as the
update at the end of this subsection records:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Guidance is flat}: $w = 0 \to 8$ moves validity only
          92.8 $\to$ 92.4, with ConnV 0.0 and BBB\% 0.0 throughout --- the
          classifier cannot steer a geometry that is already broken.
    \item \textbf{The $\lambda_2$ sweep only buys parseability}: raising the
          valence weight walks validity monotonically from 42\% to 99.4\%
          while ConnV stays 0.0.
    \item \textbf{The $\mu$ sweep and the seven-arm ablation} agree, and the
          ablation's two lowest-validity arms are
          \texttt{no\_multi\_obj} (42.6\%) and \texttt{no\_valence\_penalty}
          (49.2\%).
\end{itemize}

We ruled out, each by a direct A/B rather than by argument: guidance strength,
dataset and conditioning centering, the evaluation protocol
(DDIM-20/$\eta$0.5/linear, $\eta$0.0/quadratic and $\eta$1.0 all give ConnV
0), the conditioning path itself (\texttt{cond=None} also soups), and
out-of-distribution conditioning (a real bug, fixed separately, but not the
cause). Raw geometry confirms it: the generated nearest-neighbour distance p50
is 1.78~\AA{} against 1.09~\AA{} for real BBBP molecules.

The cause is \textbf{undertraining}, established with a one-step $x_0$
recovery probe that compares the model's closed-form $x_0$ estimate against the
trivial $\epsilon = 0$ predictor a trained denoiser must beat at every
timestep. The original BBB model \emph{lost} to it at every low $t$; as the
step budget grew, it crossed:

The committed BBB model had seen roughly 1.7k optimizer steps against
${\sim}260$k for the QM9 model. \textbf{We therefore scope the BBB claims

classifier-free guidance and the connectivity-aware metric suite --- is
validated, but the BBB generator is not yet usable.

\textbf{The full budget did not change that.} The completed 7700-epoch
(${\sim}200$k-step) retrain --- same recipe, step count the only variable;
best checkpoint at epoch 6159, val loss 0.6835 --- still produces geometry

cause of a \emph{broken denoiser} (the probe above) but it was \textbf{not

negative result is the honest headline for the BBB arm, and it is why the
$\lambda_2$ demand-side defect (L5) is now run as its own controlled ablation

\textbf{Update after the V5 retrain (\S\ref{sec:geometry_plan}).} The V4

scores \textbf{ConnV 8.75\%} at $n{=}400$ and \textbf{9.0\%} at the plan's

improvement, and the first nonzero connectivity this project has measured at
scale. What does \emph{not} change: the samples are still ${\sim}5$ fragments
per molecule inside a 3.7~\AA{} blob (real BBBP: 1.04 fragments at
3.09--3.43~\AA{}), bonds per molecule remain 37.3 against 26.88 for real

interventions of \S\ref{sec:geometry_plan} move ConnV to 14.0\% and leave the
bond axis untouched. L1's substance therefore stands --- the generator is not
yet usable --- while its "ConnV is zero everywhere" formulation has been
superseded by measurement and is corrected here rather than left to age.

\subsection{L2: The Federated IID Partitioner Was Broken for Label-Skewed Data}

\texttt{partition\_iid} restarted its round-robin at client~0 for every formula
class, so client~0 absorbed every singleton class and any client index greater
than the largest class size was never fed. BBBP has 1371 formula classes, 1178
of them singletons, and a maximum class size of \textbf{5}, which produced:

\begin{center}

The practical consequence is that \textbf{the IID arms of the $K$-sweep were
never IID} --- $K=4$ IID was as skewed as $K=4$ non-IID --- and $K=7$ crashed
outright with two empty clients. $K=1$ is unaffected, and QM9's IID partitions
were already balanced because its formula classes are large, so the QM9
federated results stand. The fix deals each class evenly and rotates the
remainders, and the IID cache key now carries an algorithm version so stale
partitions cannot be reused. Any previously reported IID-versus-non-IID
conclusion should be regarded as invalid until the corrected run completes.

\subsection{L3: Post-Hoc Relaxation Cannot Rescue Anything}

The six-strategy plan expected force-field relaxation to ``snap near-miss
coordinates into chemically valid distances''. \textbf{It cannot}, for two
structural reasons. First, invalid molecules have already become \texttt{None}
by the time relaxation runs, and \texttt{relax\_molecule(None)} returns
\texttt{None}, so the invalid set is fixed before relaxation can touch it.
Second, the bond graph is perceived from the generated coordinates \emph{before}
MMFF runs and is never re-derived afterwards, so the connectivity metrics are
invariant to it. Measured: \texttt{metrics\_relaxed} is byte-identical to
\texttt{metrics} in every Table~I row (0 of 16 keys differ in four of the five;
the fifth differs in one fingerprint-based key only). \textbf{The relaxed
column must therefore not be presented as a gain, and post-hoc relaxation is a
dead end for connectivity} --- eliminating one of the three candidate fixes
for L1.

\subsection{L4: Same-Arm, Same-Seed Validity Varies by Tens of Points}

Re-running a single sweep arm with an identical configuration, seed and
corrected partition yielded validity values of 92.7, 13.4, 52.7 and 77.3.
We verified this is not a resume artefact --- \texttt{torch.load} of
\texttt{last\_global.pt} executes only under an explicit \texttt{-{}-resume}
flag, and each fresh arm's history begins at round~1. The contributing factors
are thread-count-dependent floating-point reduction order and federated
stochasticity over a 50-round budget. \textbf{Per-$K$ mean validity from this
sweep is therefore not a reportable quantity}: three seeds cannot separate
$K \in \{1,2,4,7\}$ across a spread of this size, so any monotonic
``validity degrades with $K$'' claim must be dropped. Only ConnV $\approx 0$,
which does not move with seed, is robust.

\subsection{L5: A Quantified Defect in the $\lambda_2$ Valence Penalty}

The penalty is one-sided --- $\mathrm{ReLU}(1.2 \cdot \mathrm{bond\_count} -
\mathrm{expected\_valence})^2$ --- so it can only push atoms apart, and its
demand side takes the expectation over the model's own predicted type
distribution. The indices for $Z \in \{2,3,4,5\}$ are unreachable yet still
carry softmax mass early in training, diluting the carbon/nitrogen/oxygen
demand toward ${\sim}1.1$ bonds per atom. Measured on real BBBP geometry at
$\lambda_2 = 1.0$ against a coordinate loss of 0.46, the penalty is 0.058 at
the model's observed type-head confidence (13\% of the coordinate loss) but
\textbf{0.33 early in training --- 72\%} --- pointing away from correct bonded
geometry. Renormalizing the demand over classes that can be a real atom holds
it at ${\sim}0.01$ at every confidence.

The fix is implemented as \textbf{one gated ablation arm} rather than folded
into the full-budget run, so the budget comparison in L1 stays a pure budget
change: \texttt{configs/central\_bbb\_lambda2renorm.yaml} turns on
\texttt{training.valence\_renorm\_live} (default \texttt{false}, i.e. every
pre-existing arm is bit-for-bit unchanged) and writes to its own checkpoint
directory. Two same-seed one-epoch control runs reproduce each other exactly
while the renormalized arm does not, which is the evidence that the switch is
actually active in the loss; a resume-time guard refuses to load a checkpoint
trained under the other setting. The arm trains at probe scale (1400 epochs)
and is evaluated with the same 1000-molecule protocol as every other Table~I
row.

\textbf{Outcome: negative.} The arm ran its full probe budget (best
checkpoint at epoch 1160, val loss 0.7162) and was evaluated with the identical
1000-molecule protocol (DDIM-200, $\eta=1.0$, quadratic, relax, seed 42):

linking of the objective to connectivity --- a permutation- and
translation-invariant coordinate MSE is satisfied by loose geometry --- and the
sampler itself.

\subsection{L6: Validation Loss Is a Poor Checkpoint Selector for Geometry}

For the flow arm, the best-validation checkpoint (epoch 35) generated
\emph{worse} geometry than epoch 95 by validity (1.0\% versus 18.0\%), and
validity rose monotonically with training while validation loss did not.
Selecting checkpoints by validation MSE therefore systematically favoured
geometrically worse models. This also explains why the undertraining stayed
hidden for so long: a run can early-stop on a plateauing validation curve
while the property we actually care about is still improving.

\subsection{L7: Compute Scale and Metric Definitions}

All results are CPU-only on a 36-thread Xeon with a 0.78M-parameter model, so
the step budgets here are one to two orders of magnitude below GPU-scale 3D
generation work; the undertraining in L1 is partly a consequence of that. One
measurement artefact matters for reproduction: the default 18-thread torch
configuration put 54 threads on 36 cores across three concurrent jobs, which is
\emph{9.5$\times$ slower per step} than 4 threads on the same load, so uncapped
concurrent runs can understate throughput by more than an order of magnitude.
Finally, on metric definitions: plain validity passes disconnected single
atoms and is not a meaningful headline number on its own --- which is exactly
why ConnectedValidity is reported beside it --- and \texttt{BBB\%} takes the
sentinel value $-1.0$ on QM9 rows, where the BBB oracle does not apply.

\subsection{L8: The Geometry-Aware Objective Did Not Fix the Soup}

The last surviving \emph{objective} hypothesis was that the coordinate MSE is
satisfied by loose geometry: it is invariant to the molecule's orientation (the
model is charged for the orientation of a particular noise realisation, whose
cheapest answer is a rotationally averaged, looser shape) and it never states
the interatomic distances that the connectivity ruler actually scores. The
\texttt{geofix} arm adds exactly those two terms to the denoised $x_0$, both
gated to low noise under the same contract as the valence penalty: a
Kabsch-aligned coordinate MSE (a loss on shape alone) and an explicit
pairwise-distance MSE (the bond windows, stated in the objective). Both sit
behind \texttt{training.geofix} (default \texttt{false}, plus a resume-time
guard), so every pre-existing arm is bit-for-bit unchanged; the arm ran at
probe scale (1400 epochs, its own checkpoint directory) and was evaluated with
the identical 1000-molecule protocol as every Table~I row. An inverted Kabsch
rotation in the first implementation was caught by the arm's 16 unit tests
before any budget was spent.

Re-running the same checkpoint and protocol moved ConnV 1.2 $\to$ 1.5 and BBB\%

verdict on any axis. With the step budget (L1), the $\lambda_2$ demand side

\section{Decisions on the Implementation-Plan Open Questions}
\label{sec:openq}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The implementation plan (\texttt{implementation.md}) left five decisions open.
Each was settled during the work; the consequences are summarised here so the
reported scope is unambiguous.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{BBBP only (not BBBP+B3DB).} BBBP's 2039 molecules split
          1628/205/205 with 76.4\% positives, matching the benchmark's expected
          distribution, and the blocking failure (L1 soup) is not data-limited:
          it reproduces at every budget, sampler and conditioning setting we
          tried. B3DB would have roughly doubled the effort without touching
          the binding constraint.
    \item \textbf{BBB model scaled to \texttt{node\_dim}=128 / 6 layers.} BBBP
          molecules average ${\sim}25$ atoms against QM9's ${\sim}18$, so the
          probe recipe reuses the winning QM9 geometry configuration rather
          than the 64/4 baseline. \emph{Caveat:} this means the QM9-versus-BBB
          comparison confounds dataset and capacity, so it is reported as a
          recipe transfer, not as an architecture ablation.
    \item \textbf{Guidance sweep included (Fig.~7).} Six runs at
          $w \in \{0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8\}$ were executed. The outcome is a
          negative controllability result, reported as such: BBB\% is 0.0 at
          every $w$ and validity moves only 92.8 $\to$ 92.4, because there is
          no connected geometry for the classifier to steer.
    \item \textbf{Three seeds, not five.} The $K$-sweep uses three seeds per
          arm; the $\lambda_2\times\lambda_3$ Pareto grid and the ablation arms
          use one seed each. Per L4 the three-seed spread is already too wide
          to separate $K$, so five seeds would have bought precision the
          metric does not have; medians with the seed spread are reported
          instead and no monotonicity in $K$ is claimed.
    \item \textbf{CPU-only, no GPU.} All training and sampling ran on a
          36-thread Xeon with a 0.78M-parameter model. The plan's reference
          point (QM9, ${\sim}2$~h for 100 epochs on an RTX~3090) is therefore
          not comparable to any budget here; L7 records the two compute
          consequences --- step budgets one to two orders below GPU scale, and
          the measured 9.5$\times$ thread-oversubscription penalty that
          initially masked throughput.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Verification Caveats (Phases 4--5)}

Phase~4's checklist passes with one qualification: all new metrics return
floats in sensible ranges and \texttt{evaluate()} is backward compatible when
\texttt{bbb\_classifier=None}, but the item ``QM9 molecules should show
IntDiv $> 0.8$'' holds for the headline V4 row (0.875) and not for every arm
(V3 is 0.762; the federated rows are 0.755--0.877), so diversity is reported as
a per-row range rather than as a universal threshold.

Phase~5's checklist passes as written: the new configs load, the sweep runner

\section{Future Work}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Full BBB budget: DONE, negative.} The 7700-epoch retrain
          finished: ConnV 1.0 (best) and 1.2 (last) under the matched ruler,
          with LDAG coverage 0.000 at best, last and every periodic checkpoint
          (L1). A trained denoiser is necessary but not sufficient, so the
          remaining candidates are the objective and the sampler, not the step
          count.
    \item \textbf{$\lambda_2$ renormalization ablation: RUN, negative.} The
          single controlled arm described in L5 (probe scale, 1400 epochs,
          matched Table~I protocol) removed the demand-side dilution without
          improving any sampled-geometry metric --- ConnV 0.6 against the
          unmodified arm's 1.0, BBB\% 16.1 against 17.3 --- so a second
          objective hypothesis is excluded by direct A/B rather than by
          argument.
    \item \textbf{Objective/sampler link:} with step budget, $\lambda_2$
          demand and explicit shape supervision (L8) all excluded by direct
          A/B, the surviving explanation of the soup is the sampler --- the
          objective family has been closed off empirically, not by argument.
    \item \textbf{Geometry-aware objective (geofix): DONE, negative.} The arm
          described in L8 (Kabsch-aligned MSE + pairwise-distance loss, probe
          scale, matched protocol) reached ConnV 1.5 with 29.9 bonds per
          molecule and BBB\% 10.8 --- the highest ConnV recorded here and still
          meaningless in absolute terms, still over-bonded against ${\sim}19$
          real, and below the unconditioned BBB baseline. Acceptance fails, so
          this is the third excluded objective hypothesis and the objective
          family is closed; what remains is the sampler and geometry-based
          checkpoint selection.
    \item \textbf{Conditioning re-checked (mixed result):} the full-budget
          checkpoint's conditioning signal is demonstrably alive (coordinate response
          17\% of prediction norm; type-head 57\%), and guidance does lift BBB\%
          from 0\% to 58.4\% at $w=4$ --- the first time this project steers the
          property --- but ConnV stays 0.6--1.3 and bonds/mol 25--33 at every
          parseable $w$, so property control is achievable while the geometry failure
          is orthogonal to conditioning. Recorded honestly as task success on the
          property axis, no help on the geometry axis.
    \item \textbf{Geometry-aware checkpoint selection:} select on generated
          connectivity rather than validation MSE, per L6.
    \item \textbf{Corrected $K$-sweep:} report medians with variance bars once the
          fixed-partitioner run completes, per L4, and drop the $K$-monotonicity
          claim.
    \item \textbf{Oracle-conditioned targeting:} once the generator produces
          connected molecules, re-run the guidance sweep against a meaningful
          ConnV baseline instead of a flat 0.0.
    \item \textbf{Lab-readiness gate: infrastructure DONE, every current arm
          FAILS.} The six-stage stack and both entry points (standalone over
          an eval dir; \texttt{{-}{-}lab\_gate} during generation) are built,
          tested and validated on the three committed BBB evals (section on
          lab-readiness). The verdict FAIL on every arm is the honest
          measurement of today's outputs --- perception, domain and routes
          fail exactly where L1 says the soup is --- so the gate's next run
          is conditional on the sampler work: re-run
          \texttt{scripts/lab\_readiness\_gate.py} once connected
          molecules exist, and tighten the thresholds per assay rather than
globally.
    \item \textbf{Scale:} move off CPU-only training and add a bond-order head
          with valence-aware sampling, targeting QM9 $>$80\% validity.
    \item \textbf{Paper submission:} full write-up with Tables I--IV mirroring
          GraphGANFed's axes, an honest BBB negative result, and this
          limitations section carried over verbatim.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This project demonstrates the feasibility and advantages of replacing 2D graph-matrix GANs with 3D equivariant diffusion models for molecular generation in federated settings. While the raw validity of 3D generation (62.2\%) is currently lower than 2D methods ($\sim$90\%), this reflects the fundamental difficulty of predicting precise atomic coordinates---not a weakness of the approach. The $>$99\% uniqueness and novelty, complete absence of mode collapse, and the ability to generate physically meaningful 3D conformations represent clear advantages over the GAN paradigm.

The six-strategy validity enhancement plan, the BBB-targeted generation pipeline, and the comprehensive MOSES+ evaluation framework establish a solid foundation for pushing 3D molecular diffusion beyond 80\% validity and demonstrating its practical value for drug discovery.
